# Sustainability in Everyday Life

## Framing
Sustainability means meeting present needs without eroding the ability of future generations to
meet theirs. In practice it is about material and energy flows: what we extract, how long it stays
in use, and where it ends up. A circular economy keeps materials in use and designs waste out
instead of managing it at the end.

## Where personal emissions actually come from
In most high-income countries the ranking is roughly:
1. Transport, especially flying and daily car use.
2. Home energy, dominated by heating and cooling.
3. Diet, dominated by beef, lamb and dairy.
4. Everything else: goods, clothing, electronics, services.

A single long-haul return flight can exceed 1 tCO2e per passenger. Beef produces roughly 10-50x the
emissions of pulses per gram of protein. Retiring a petrol car early for an EV pays back its
manufacturing footprint typically within 1-2 years of average driving in a moderately clean grid.

## High-leverage actions, ranked
- Switch home heating to a heat pump and insulate first.
- Drive less and fly less; replace the trips you can with rail or video.
- Shift diet toward plants; you do not have to be vegan for it to count.
- Buy less and keep things longer, especially clothing and electronics.
- Move your pension and savings out of fossil-heavy funds. This often dwarfs every other item.
- Use your professional and civic leverage: procurement decisions, planning meetings, votes.

## Where effort is often wasted
- Obsessing over straws and bags while ignoring flights and heating.
- Buying new "eco" versions of things that still work. The greenest product is the one you own.
- Carbon offsets used as a substitute for cuts. Quality varies wildly and permanence is hard.
- Recycling as a first resort rather than a last one.

## Talking about it
Evidence suggests social contagion matters: visible solar panels, visible EVs and visible dietary
change all raise neighbours' adoption. Talking about what you did and why is a genuine multiplier,
and it works better than talking about what other people should do.
