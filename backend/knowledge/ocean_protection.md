# Ocean Protection

## In one paragraph
The ocean covers 71% of the planet, produces roughly half of the oxygen we breathe, has absorbed
more than 90% of the excess heat trapped by greenhouse gases and about a quarter of our CO2
emissions. That service has come at the cost of warming, acidification and deoxygenation. Only
about 8% of the ocean has any protected status, and a much smaller share is fully protected.

## Pressures
1. Overfishing: roughly 35-38% of assessed fish stocks are harvested beyond biologically
   sustainable levels (FAO). Bottom trawling flattens seafloor habitat and resuspends carbon.
2. Warming: marine heatwaves have roughly doubled in frequency since the 1980s. The bleaching
   event that began in 2023 affected reefs in more than 80 countries.
3. Acidification: surface pH has fallen about 0.1 units since pre-industrial times, a ~30%
   increase in hydrogen ion concentration, which makes shell building harder.
4. Deoxygenation: open-ocean oxygen has declined about 2% since 1960 and low-oxygen zones are expanding.
5. Pollution: plastics, nutrient runoff, oil, and chronic shipping noise.
6. Coastal destruction: mangroves, seagrass and salt marsh cleared for aquaculture and development.

## Why it matters
- Around 3 billion people rely on seafood as a significant protein source.
- Coral reefs support about a quarter of marine species on less than 1% of the seafloor.
- Blue carbon habitats store carbon per hectare at several times the rate of tropical forest and
  buffer coasts against storm surge.
- Ocean circulation moves heat around the planet; changes to it reshape regional climate.

## Solutions that work
- Fully or highly protected marine reserves. Fish biomass inside well-enforced reserves typically
  rises several fold, with spillover benefits to adjacent fisheries.
- The High Seas Treaty (BBNJ), which finally creates a legal route to protect areas beyond national
  jurisdiction. It needs ratification and funding to matter.
- Ending harmful fishing subsidies and requiring transparent vessel tracking to curb illegal fishing.
- Science-based catch limits, gear restrictions and bycatch reduction devices.
- Mangrove, seagrass and salt marsh restoration as combined carbon, fisheries and flood defence.
- Cutting emissions: no marine protection strategy survives unchecked warming and acidification.

## Practical advice
Check a local seafood guide before buying; the species and the fishing method both matter, and
trawled or dredged products carry the heaviest habitat cost. Support reserve designations locally,
where fishing councils and coastal planning decisions are actually made.
