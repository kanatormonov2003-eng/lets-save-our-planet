# Biodiversity Loss

## In one paragraph
Biodiversity is the variety of life at gene, species and ecosystem level, and it underwrites food,
medicine, clean water and climate stability. Monitored vertebrate populations have declined by an
average of about 73% since 1970 (WWF Living Planet Index, 2024), and more than 47,000 assessed
species are threatened with extinction (IUCN Red List). Extinction rates are estimated at tens to
hundreds of times the natural background rate.

## The five drivers (IPBES ranking)
1. Land and sea use change: habitat conversion, mostly for agriculture. The largest driver on land.
2. Direct exploitation: overfishing, logging, hunting and the wildlife trade. The largest at sea.
3. Climate change: shifting ranges, phenological mismatch, coral bleaching, and moving faster than
   many species can adapt or migrate.
4. Pollution: pesticides, nutrient runoff, plastics, light and noise.
5. Invasive alien species, moved by trade and travel, especially destructive on islands.

## Consequences
- Pollination: about 75% of leading food crop types benefit from animal pollination.
- Resilience: simplified ecosystems recover badly from drought, fire, storms and disease.
- Health: habitat fragmentation increases contact between wildlife, livestock and people and
  raises zoonotic spillover risk.
- Fisheries and forestry livelihoods collapse when the underlying stock does.
- Genetic erosion narrows the raw material for crop breeding and pharmaceutical discovery.

## Solutions that work
- The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework: protect 30% of land and sea by 2030 and
  restore 30% of degraded ecosystems. Signed by 196 parties in 2022.
- Effective, funded and locally governed protected areas rather than paper parks.
- Habitat corridors that reconnect fragmented populations.
- Agricultural reform: hedgerows, field margins, reduced pesticide load, integrated pest management,
  and subsidy reform that pays for outcomes rather than volume.
- Rewilding and keystone species reintroduction; wolves in Yellowstone and beavers across Europe
  changed whole river systems.
- Biosecurity to stop invasive introductions, which is far cheaper than eradication.

## Encouraging evidence
Targeted conservation works. The European bison, the humpback whale, the Iberian lynx and the
European otter all recovered from near collapse. Species recovery is slow and expensive but it is
not hypothetical.
