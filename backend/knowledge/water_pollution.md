# Water Pollution

## In one paragraph
About 80% of the world's wastewater is released to the environment without adequate treatment.
Rivers concentrate nutrients, pathogens, metals and synthetic chemicals and deliver them to lakes,
deltas and coastal seas. Roughly 2 billion people use a drinking water source contaminated with
faeces (WHO/UNICEF).

## Main categories
- Nutrient pollution: nitrogen and phosphorus from fertiliser, manure and sewage.
- Pathogens: bacteria, viruses and parasites from untreated human and animal waste.
- Industrial chemicals: heavy metals, solvents, dyes, PFAS "forever chemicals".
- Thermal pollution: warm cooling water lowers dissolved oxygen.
- Sediment: erosion from farming, logging and construction smothers riverbeds and reefs.
- Pharmaceuticals and microplastics, which conventional treatment was never designed to remove.

## Causes
1. Over-application of fertiliser; roughly half of applied nitrogen is never taken up by crops.
2. Missing or overloaded sewage treatment, plus combined sewer overflows during heavy rain.
3. Weak enforcement of industrial discharge limits.
4. Mining tailings failures and acid mine drainage.
5. Urban runoff carrying oil, tyre particles, metals and road salt.

## Consequences
- Eutrophication and hypoxic dead zones. The Gulf of Mexico zone regularly exceeds 15,000 km2.
- Harmful algal blooms that shut down drinking water supplies and fisheries.
- Waterborne disease: diarrhoeal illness remains a leading cause of child mortality.
- Bioaccumulation: mercury and PFAS concentrate up the food chain into fish and people.
- Loss of freshwater biodiversity, which has declined faster than terrestrial or marine.

## Solutions that work
- Universal wastewater treatment with nutrient recovery; phosphorus is a finite resource worth reclaiming.
- Precision agriculture, cover crops, buffer strips and restored wetlands to intercept runoff.
- Strict PFAS and heavy metal limits at the point of discharge with public monitoring data.
- Separate storm and foul sewers, and use permeable surfaces and rain gardens to cut peak flows.
- Protect source watersheds: New York City avoided a multi-billion dollar filtration plant by
  investing in the Catskill watershed instead.

## Practical advice
Do not flush medicines or wet wipes. Use phosphate-free detergent. If you have a garden, fertilise
by soil test rather than by habit, and leave an unmown strip along any watercourse.
