# Climate Change

## In one paragraph
Human activity has raised the concentration of heat-trapping gases in the atmosphere far above
anything seen in at least 800,000 years. Atmospheric CO2 rose from about 280 ppm before the
industrial revolution to roughly 424 ppm in 2024. The resulting energy imbalance has warmed the
global surface by about 1.3 C relative to 1850-1900, and 2024 was the first calendar year to
average above 1.5 C.

## Key figures
- CO2 concentration: ~424 ppm (2024 annual average, NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory).
- Warming: ~1.55 C above the 1850-1900 baseline in 2024 (WMO); the long-term trend is ~1.3 C.
- Global CO2 emissions from fossil fuels: ~37-38 GtCO2 a year (Global Carbon Project).
- Sea level rise: ~4.3 mm a year over the satellite era, and accelerating.
- Remaining 1.5 C carbon budget: a few hundred GtCO2, i.e. under a decade at current emissions.

## Causes
1. Fossil fuel combustion (coal, oil, gas) for electricity, industry, heating and transport.
   This is roughly three quarters of global greenhouse gas emissions.
2. Methane: leaks from oil and gas infrastructure, coal mines, landfills, rice paddies and
   enteric fermentation in ruminants. About 80x the warming power of CO2 over 20 years.
3. Land-use change: deforestation, peat drainage and soil degradation release stored carbon.
4. Industrial process emissions: cement clinker and steel emit CO2 through chemistry, not just fuel.
5. Nitrous oxide from fertiliser use, with an atmospheric lifetime above a century.

## Consequences
- Heat: events that were once-in-fifty-year extremes now occur several times per decade.
- Water: heavier downpours where it rains, deeper droughts where it does not.
- Ice: Greenland and Antarctica are losing mass; most mountain glaciers are in retreat.
- Oceans: warming, acidification (surface pH down ~0.1 since pre-industrial) and marine heatwaves.
- Food and health: yield losses in staple crops, expanded ranges for dengue and malaria vectors.
- Justice: the countries that emitted least are hit hardest and have the least adaptation capital.

## Solutions that work
- Decarbonise electricity: wind and solar are now the cheapest new generation in most markets.
- Electrify end uses: EVs, heat pumps, induction cooking, industrial electrification.
- Cut methane fast: leak detection and repair is cheap and gives near-term temperature benefit.
- Efficiency and demand reduction: insulation, public transport, denser cities.
- Carbon pricing plus removal of fossil subsidies (~7 trillion USD a year including implicit costs, IMF).
- Protect and restore natural carbon sinks; they are complements to cutting emissions, not substitutes.

## Common misconceptions
- "The climate always changed." It did, but not this fast and not while 8 billion people depended
  on fixed coastlines, fixed farmland and fixed water supplies.
- "1.5 C is a cliff edge." It is not a switch, it is a dial. Every tenth of a degree adds risk,
  which is why overshoot still matters and why later action still counts.
- "Individual action is pointless." Individual action matters most where it changes systems:
  votes, jobs, procurement, and the norms that make policy possible.
