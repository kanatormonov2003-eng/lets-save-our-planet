# Plastic Pollution

## In one paragraph
The world produces well over 400 million tonnes of plastic a year. Only about 9% of all plastic
ever made has been recycled; the rest was landfilled, burned or lost to the environment. Roughly
8-11 million tonnes enter the ocean each year, where UV and wave action fragment it into
microplastics rather than eliminating it.

## Key figures
- Global production: >400 Mt a year and rising (OECD).
- Recycled share of all plastic ever produced: ~9%.
- Packaging is the largest single application, roughly a third of production.
- Plastics account for around 3-4% of global greenhouse gas emissions across their life cycle.
- Degradation: a PET bottle persists for centuries; it never truly disappears, it fragments.

## Causes
1. Single-use design: minutes of utility, centuries of persistence.
2. Multi-layer laminates and mixed polymers that no sorting facility can economically separate.
3. Waste collection that has not kept pace with consumption, particularly in fast-growing cities.
4. Cheap virgin resin, often subsidised, undercutting recycled feedstock.
5. Diffuse microplastic sources: synthetic textiles in laundry, tyre wear, paint, and pellet spills.

## Consequences
- More than 900 marine species are documented to ingest or become entangled in plastic.
- Microplastics are found in tap water, bottled water, salt, soil, air, human blood and placenta.
  Health effects are an active research area; exposure is now unavoidable.
- Additives such as phthalates and bisphenols leach out; several are endocrine disruptors.
- Blocked drains worsen urban flooding and create mosquito breeding habitat.
- Open burning of waste plastic releases dioxins and black carbon.

## Solutions that work
- Design out the problem: reuse systems, refill, single-polymer packaging, and less packaging.
- Extended producer responsibility, which shifts end-of-life cost onto the producer.
- Deposit return schemes: container return rates above 90% where they operate.
- Bans on the most leak-prone items where alternatives exist (thin carrier bags, cutlery, straws).
- Investment in collection in the ~1,000 rivers responsible for most ocean-bound plastic.
- Washing machine microfibre filters, mandatory in France for new machines.
- A binding global plastics treaty covering production, not only waste management.

## Common misconceptions
- "Recycling solves it." Mechanical recycling downgrades polymer quality; most plastic can be
  recycled once or twice at best. Reduction and reuse come first.
- "Bioplastic is harmless." Most compostable plastics need industrial composting at 55-60 C and
  behave like conventional plastic in the sea.
- "Ocean cleanup devices will fix it." They help in gyres and rivers but capture a tiny share;
  the leverage is upstream.
