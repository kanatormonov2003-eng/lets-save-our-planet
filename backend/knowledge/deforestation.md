# Deforestation

## In one paragraph
Forests cover about 31% of the land surface and hold more carbon than the atmosphere. Tropical
primary forest loss reached a record 6.7 million hectares in 2024, an area roughly the size of
Panama, and for the first time fire rather than agriculture was the leading driver. Parts of the
southeastern Amazon have flipped from carbon sink to carbon source.

## Key figures
- Tropical primary forest loss in 2024: 6.7 Mha (WRI / Global Forest Watch).
- Forests absorb roughly a third of annual anthropogenic CO2 emissions.
- Agriculture drives the majority of tropical deforestation: cattle, soy, palm oil, cocoa, coffee.
- Indigenous-managed territories consistently show lower deforestation rates than surrounding land.

## Causes
1. Cattle ranching, the single largest driver in the Amazon.
2. Commodity crops: soy (largely for animal feed), oil palm, cocoa, coffee, rubber.
3. Fire, both deliberate clearing and escaped fires in drought-stressed forest.
4. Illegal and unregulated logging, often followed by conversion.
5. Roads, mining and hydropower that fragment intact forest and open access.
6. Fuelwood and charcoal demand in regions without affordable alternatives.

## Consequences
- Carbon: releases stored carbon and removes future sequestration capacity.
- Rainfall: the Amazon recycles moisture that supplies farmland thousands of kilometres away.
  Forest loss reduces regional rainfall and lengthens dry seasons.
- Biodiversity: tropical forests hold well over half of terrestrial species.
- Soil and water: erosion, siltation, reduced flood buffering, lower dry-season flows.
- People: displacement of Indigenous and forest-dependent communities.
- Tipping point risk: estimates put Amazon dieback risk in the range of 20-25% cumulative loss.

## Solutions that work
- Deforestation-free supply chains verified by satellite; the EU deforestation regulation makes
  this a market access condition for several commodities.
- Recognition and enforcement of Indigenous land tenure, one of the most cost-effective
  interventions per hectare protected.
- Enforcement plus economic alternatives: Brazil cut Amazon deforestation sharply in 2004-2012 and
  again after 2023 with monitoring, fines and credit restrictions.
- Payment for ecosystem services and jurisdictional REDD+ with credible measurement.
- Restoration that prioritises natural regeneration and native species mixes; plantations store
  less carbon and support far less biodiversity than recovered natural forest.
- Demand side: less beef and dairy, verified palm oil, and cutting food waste.
